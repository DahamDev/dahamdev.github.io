# Cinematic hiker summit hero

The hero is a live Three.js environment with a personal introduction in front: “Hi, I’m Daham. Curious by nature.” The homepage brings together technical projects, writing and hiking, with conversational copy rather than a services pitch. It opens with the rear-facing hiker already standing at the cliff edge. After a two-second pause, the hiker bends at the hips and knees, braces both hands on the rock, and lowers into a seated position. The legs move over the lip one at a time before the hands settle onto the knees. The seated pose is reached at 7.8 seconds. There is no walking sequence and no trekking poles.

The camera makes a three-second rear dolly toward the hiker, holds close during the sit, then gradually rises and pans across the lake and mountain vista from seconds 20–30. The camera remains behind the character, including during manual exploration. Desktop keeps the hiker on the right of the text; portrait framing places the character below it. The camera settles into a small drift after the reveal.

Tapered clothing meshes with compression folds replace capsule-shaped arms and legs. Proportional boots have panel seams, laces and tread; the smaller olive pack has straps, buckles, a bottle and a rolled mat. Warm summer sunlight, clear blue sky fill and a tighter shadow frustum add shape to the character. The summer palette uses vivid turquoise water, fresh green hills, golden tussock and crisp snow, with reduced distance haze so the mountains retain their colour. Water and terrain share the same fog density. The landscape includes a turquoise lake with a planar scene reflection, animated waves and glints, densely tessellated erosion-shaped snowy ridges, fractured rocks, fine branch cutouts, curved grass, wildflowers and drifting clouds. Procedural material detail is generated locally; the hero does not load image files.

A small stone-ring campfire, set farther to the hiker’s side on a cleared patch of the summit, heats an enamel tea mug on a steel trivet beside the hiker. Procedural flames, glowing coals, flickering local light and rising steam share the scene clock. After sitting, the hiker reaches for the handle at 8.4 seconds, makes contact at 10 seconds, closes the fingers and thumb around the handle, pauses briefly, then lifts the upright mug along a curved path. The lift finishes at 12.6 seconds, with the mug held farther forward to avoid folding the arm against the chest. The first sip reaches the lips at 15 seconds; tipping happens from 15.05 seconds, followed by an upright pause, separation from the lips and a slower lowering motion ending at 19.3 seconds. The mug then rests in the hand between occasional sips. The camera holds close through the first sip before revealing the vista. The handle faces toward the hiker on the trivet and follows the carrying arm during the lift. The hand approaches from the hiker’s side, with a supported arc around the shoulder. An offset wrist grip and articulated fingers wrap the handle without passing through the cup; hand and forearm geometry are checked for clearance. While drinking, the near rim stays anchored to the actual mouth transform as the head moves and the cup tilts. The elbow opens outward during the reach and settles downward after the cup clears the body. Pickup elbow flexion stays below 90°, and wrist bend eases smoothly toward a limit below 80°. Asymmetric easing gives the reach, lift and lowering different pacing, while fingers and thumb close in a staggered sequence. Mug position and wrist targets share the same transform, including during replay. The tea surface stays level as the mug tilts.

Supporting palms face down with fingers toward the cliff; resting hands sit on top of the knees. Two-bone inverse kinematics keeps boots fixed during the crouch and palms anchored while supporting the descent. A waist pivot moves the torso, head, shoulders and pack together. Smooth pose tracks coordinate hip settling and staggered leg release, with small equipment settling movements, breathing and head movement. This is a procedural WebGL scene, not a motion-captured character or a prerendered game-engine cinematic.


## Controls and performance

- Drag the background to explore within a limited horizontal and vertical range. Release to ease back to the current cinematic view. The camera stays behind the hiker and above the summit. Links and copy do not initiate a drag.
- Touch dragging supports horizontal exploration while preserving vertical page scrolling and pinch zoom. Pointer cancellation, loss of capture and focus changes end the drag safely.
- Focus the hero and use arrow keys to explore; releasing the keys or pressing Escape returns the view.
- Pause/play freezes or resumes automatic scene motion; manual exploration and camera return still work while paused. The replay arrow restarts the standing-to-sitting moment and resets the camera offset.
- Reduced motion opens on a still, seated summit pose. Manual camera movement is direct, with an immediate return instead of a spring animation. Replay is an explicit opt-in to animation.
- Rendering pauses outside the viewport or in hidden tabs.
- Mobile uses fewer terrain segments and vegetation instances, a lower pixel ratio, and no character shadow map. Desktop uses a 2048-pixel character shadow map. Animation targets 60 fps with refresh-rate-independent timing. Persistently slow devices first reduce resolution and then fall back to 30 fps.
- Reflections use a 768 × 512 render target at approximately 10 fps on desktop, or 256 × 256 at 7.5 fps on mobile; their timing is independent of the main animation, so moving to 60 fps does not double reflection work.
- Phone hero height is clamped to 600–680 px (78svh), with tighter copy spacing and compact action buttons so the animation and text share the section more evenly.
- The hiker has a separate portrait framing to keep them in view below the copy. On phones, the text overlay fades out above the hiker, and the bottom-control shading is limited to the bottom 14%; there is no full-canvas mobile tint.
- `static/js/vendor/three.module.min.js` is the self-hosted Three.js 0.170.0 runtime; its MIT license is beside it.
- The hero displays a plain background until the 3D scene is ready. No image loads or appears on refresh. Disabled JavaScript, unavailable WebGL, and shader/context errors leave that plain background behind the text.

## Auckland time of day

- The hero defaults to the current Auckland time, using `Intl.DateTimeFormat` with `Pacific/Auckland` so the visitor's timezone and New Zealand daylight saving changes are handled correctly.
- An unboxed, single-line corner control shows **Auckland time**, the clock and a keyboard-accessible **Time travel** switch. Subtle text shadows maintain legibility over the scene. It is off by default and follows current Auckland time. Switching it on loops through a full day in 90 seconds, starting at the displayed time. Switching it off immediately restores live time. The switch indicates its active state, with preview status also available to screen readers. There is no slider or circular dial.
- Time travel shares the renderer's frame loop and reflection throttle. It works independently of the hiker's pause state, suspends offscreen or in hidden tabs, and resumes without fast-forwarding. Reduced-motion visitors remain still by default but can explicitly enable time travel. No additional requestAnimationFrame loop is created; simulated days do not fetch other dates. Mobile controls occupy a single compact row above the copy, with no panel background.
- Sky, clouds, water, atmospheric haze, terrain lighting and the hiker's lighting interpolate through dawn, daylight, golden hour, sunset and night. The visible sun follows a low scenic arc, and night adds subtle stars and a cratered full moon with a soft halo. Cool directional moonlight illuminates terrain and clothing, with matching silver lake glints and the existing foreground shadow map. Night shadows use a lifted blue ambient palette, with a soft, local moon-bounce fill on the camera-facing side of the hiker to reveal the jacket, backpack and nearby rock. This fill fades out through dawn and introduces no extra shadow map. The campfire remains warm. Celestial directions are shared by the sky, lights and water. These are art-directed paths and a full-moon lighting treatment, not real lunar positions or phases. There is no added full-screen color or fog overlay.
- Sunrise, sunset and civil twilight come from [SunriseSunset.io](https://sunrisesunset.io/api/) for Auckland (-36.8485, 174.7633), with an explicit local date, timezone and 24-hour format. A visible attribution link is in the homepage footer; detailed sun times remain available to screen readers. One successful daily response is cached locally; midnight triggers a new date lookup.
- Loading, blocked storage, invalid responses, timeout and network errors leave a labeled seasonal estimate active. Failed requests retry at most hourly. Stale responses cannot overwrite a newer date. The clock refreshes every 15 seconds while visible and when returning to the tab.
- The controller tests cover DST, date rollover, travel/live switching, cache reuse, API failure and stale-response handling. Time travel checks cover the 90-second cycle, repeated midnight wraparound, restoring live time, hidden-tab suspension and disposal. Renderer checks cover travel with a paused hiker, shared rendering and reflection cadence, offscreen suspension and resuming without jumps. Lighting checks cover every minute of each seasonal fallback, midnight continuity and unchanged character/camera state. Celestial checks cover sun/moon handover, night lighting, shared reflection directions, default camera framing and mountain occlusion at midday/midnight.
- The live API probe returned HTTP 403 from this editing environment. API integration was checked against the documented contract with synthetic fixtures; a successful production API response and browser/GPU appearance were not verified here.

Full-sequence checks verify pickup elbow flexion, wrist bend, hand/forearm clearance from the jacket and smooth joint acceleration. Interaction checks cover drag and return while paused, pointer capture and cancellation, excluded controls and text, reduced motion, keyboard controls, bounded camera positions and smooth return to the default view. Sitting tests also check that palms face down, fingers point forward, and hand geometry clears the rock. A 60 Hz motion audit checks foot targets, stationary stance contacts, planted palms, boot clearance and pelvis continuity throughout the sequence.

Run `hugo --minify` and `node --test tests/alpine.test.mjs tests/daylight.test.mjs` with Node 22+. Shader stages were also parsed after expanding Three.js includes and preprocessing. Browser/GPU visual verification was unavailable in this editing session; the tests do not claim to validate GPU output. Offline geometry projections were used to inspect composition and the reach, sip and resting poses.
