# Cinematic hiker summit hero

The hero is a live Three.js environment with a personal introduction in front: “Hi, I’m Daham. Curious by nature.” The homepage brings together technical projects, writing and hiking, with conversational copy rather than a services pitch. It opens with the rear-facing hiker already standing at the cliff edge. After a two-second pause, the hiker bends at the hips and knees, braces both hands on the rock, and lowers into a seated position. The legs move over the lip one at a time before the hands settle onto the knees. The seated pose is reached at 7.8 seconds. There is no walking sequence and no trekking poles.

The camera makes a three-second rear dolly toward the hiker, holds close during the sit, then gradually rises and pans across the lake and mountain vista from seconds 20–30. It remains behind the character. Desktop keeps the hiker on the right of the text; portrait framing places the character below it. The camera settles into a small drift after the reveal.

Tapered clothing meshes with compression folds replace capsule-shaped arms and legs. Proportional boots have panel seams, laces and tread; the smaller olive pack has straps, buckles, a bottle and a rolled mat. Warm directional light, cool sky fill and a tighter shadow frustum add shape to the character. The landscape includes a turquoise lake with a planar scene reflection, animated waves and glints, densely tessellated erosion-shaped snowy ridges, fractured rocks, fine branch cutouts, curved grass, wildflowers and drifting clouds. Procedural material detail is generated locally; the hero does not load image files.

A small stone-ring campfire, set farther to the hiker’s side on a cleared patch of the summit, heats an enamel tea mug on a steel trivet beside the hiker. Procedural flames, glowing coals, flickering local light and rising steam share the scene clock. After sitting, the hiker reaches for the handle at 8.4 seconds, makes contact at 10 seconds, closes the fingers and thumb around the handle, pauses briefly, then lifts the upright mug along a curved path. The lift finishes at 12.6 seconds, with the mug held farther forward to avoid folding the arm against the chest. The first sip reaches the lips at 15 seconds; tipping happens from 15.05 seconds, followed by an upright pause, separation from the lips and a slower lowering motion ending at 19.3 seconds. The mug then rests in the hand between occasional sips. The camera holds close through the first sip before revealing the vista. The handle faces toward the hiker on the trivet and follows the carrying arm during the lift. The hand approaches from the hiker’s side, with a supported arc around the shoulder. An offset wrist grip and articulated fingers wrap the handle without passing through the cup; hand and forearm geometry are checked for clearance. While drinking, the near rim stays anchored to the actual mouth transform as the head moves and the cup tilts. The elbow opens outward during the reach and settles downward after the cup clears the body. Pickup elbow flexion stays below 90°, and wrist bend eases smoothly toward a limit below 80°. Asymmetric easing gives the reach, lift and lowering different pacing, while fingers and thumb close in a staggered sequence. Mug position and wrist targets share the same transform, including during replay. The tea surface stays level as the mug tilts.

Supporting palms face down with fingers toward the cliff; resting hands sit on top of the knees. Two-bone inverse kinematics keeps boots fixed during the crouch and palms anchored while supporting the descent. A waist pivot moves the torso, head, shoulders and pack together. Smooth pose tracks coordinate hip settling and staggered leg release, with small equipment settling movements, breathing and head movement. This is a procedural WebGL scene, not a motion-captured character or a prerendered game-engine cinematic.


## Controls and performance

- Pause/play freezes or resumes the entire scene. The replay arrow restarts the standing-to-sitting moment.
- Reduced motion opens on a still, seated summit pose. Replay is an explicit opt-in to animation.
- Rendering pauses outside the viewport or in hidden tabs.
- Mobile uses fewer terrain segments and vegetation instances, a lower pixel ratio, and no character shadow map. Desktop uses a 2048-pixel character shadow map. Animation targets 60 fps with refresh-rate-independent timing. Persistently slow devices first reduce resolution and then fall back to 30 fps.
- Reflections use a 768 × 512 render target at approximately 10 fps on desktop, or 256 × 256 at 7.5 fps on mobile; their timing is independent of the main animation, so moving to 60 fps does not double reflection work.
- Phone hero height is clamped to 600–680 px (78svh), with tighter copy spacing and compact action buttons so the animation and text share the section more evenly.
- The hiker has a separate portrait framing to keep them in view below the copy. On phones, the text overlay fades out above the hiker, and the bottom-control shading is limited to the bottom 14%; there is no full-canvas mobile tint.
- `static/js/vendor/three.module.min.js` is the self-hosted Three.js 0.170.0 runtime; its MIT license is beside it.
- The hero displays a plain background until the 3D scene is ready. No image loads or appears on refresh. Disabled JavaScript, unavailable WebGL, and shader/context errors leave that plain background behind the text.

## Files and verification

- `themes/portfolio/layouts/index.html`: hero markup and controls.
- `themes/portfolio/assets/js/alpine.js`: character, terrain, shaders, animation and lifecycle.
- `themes/portfolio/assets/css/styles.css`: light theme and responsive overlay.
- `tests/alpine.test.mjs`: seventeen geometry and lifecycle checks, including the standing/sitting transition, close camera approach and vista reveal, rear camera framing, pause/replay, reduced motion fallback behavior, and reflection camera/render-state restoration. Tea interaction checks cover handle contact, cup/hand continuity, precise rim-to-lip contact throughout tipping, upright pickup, elbow height, joint rotation continuity, handle direction, full hand/forearm clearance from the cup and replay reset over 60 seconds. Full-sequence checks verify pickup elbow flexion, wrist bend, hand/forearm clearance from the jacket and smooth joint acceleration. Renderer checks cover 60/120 Hz displays, the separate reflection cadence and slow-device fallback. Sitting tests also check that palms face down, fingers point forward, and hand geometry clears the rock. A 60 Hz motion audit checks foot targets, stationary stance contacts, planted palms, boot clearance and pelvis continuity throughout the sequence.

Run `hugo --minify` and `node --test tests/alpine.test.mjs` with Node 22+. Shader stages were also parsed after expanding Three.js includes and preprocessing. Browser/GPU visual verification was unavailable in this editing session; the tests do not claim to validate GPU output. Offline geometry projections were used to inspect composition and the reach, sip and resting poses.
